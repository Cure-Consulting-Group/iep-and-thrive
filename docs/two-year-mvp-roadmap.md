## The MVP reframe

"Think MVP" changes the answer, and it should.

Everything to this point has been building toward **a school in September 2028** — facility, Regents charter, ~$1M, 16 students. That is not an MVP. That is the Series A.

**The MVP is a certified-provider practice with no facility.**

| | The school (Series A) | **The practice (MVP)** |
|---|---|---|
| Facility | Leased, certified, C-of-O for school use | **None.** Delivered in-school, in-home, or in rented hourly space |
| Entity | 501(c)(3) + Regents charter | **Simple NY nonprofit** — charter deferred until there's a school |
| Capital | ~$800K–$1.5M | **<$25K** |
| Staff | Head of school, admissions, teachers | **Tiffany + 2–3 contracted certified interventionists** |
| Revenue | Placement funding | **Summer private-pay + school-year vouchers** |
| Risk if wrong | Catastrophic | **A year and $25K** |

The practice proves the four things the school decision actually turns on: **do the outcomes hold up, will attorneys refer, do the voucher mechanics work, and can Tiffany do this alongside the day job.** Answer those with real data and real cash, then decide about a school in mid-2028.

> **Entity note.** Don't file the Regents charter petition in year one. A Regents charter incorporates a *school*; a provider practice may not need one. **[VERIFY with counsel]** which entity form the practice actually requires under Educ. Law §216 / N-PCL §404(d) — it may be materially simpler and faster than the charter route.

## The two tracks, and why they feed each other

The Dean job is not something to survive for two years. Played deliberately, it is **the fastest way to build the credential the venture needs** — and it raises the salary floor Tiffany would eventually leave from.

| | **Dean track** | **Venture track** | **How they compound** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Y1 2026-27** | Dean, expanding into IEP oversight and CSE liaison scope | Evidence + entity + summer v1 | More CSE table time = more testimony credibility, a deeper referral network, and the exact experience the promotion requires |
| **Y2 2027-28** | **Director of Special Education / Student Support** | Practice launches, first public revenue | Supervisory and budget ownership are prerequisites for an eventual Educational Director role *and* for the 853 fiscal application |

### The Y1 ask at the charter

Volunteer into **IEP oversight, CSE liaison, and SPED compliance** — the scope nobody wants and everyone needs. It is the highest-leverage unpaid work available, because it simultaneously: builds the promotion case, puts Tiffany at more CSE tables, deepens the district network, and generates exactly the supervisory record a NYSED reviewer will look for in 2029.

Position for the title at the **spring 2027 review**, not the following fall.

### On SBL certification

**Confirm first whether Tiffany already holds School Building Leader certification.** If yes, nothing to do. If no: **pursue it only if the charter funds it and it's already on the promotion path.** For a school-age 853 program, §200.7(b)(6) requires Part 80 certification for instructional and supervisory personnel — the SPED teaching certificate she presumably holds. A part-time CAS program would consume the same 12 hours a week the venture needs, and the trade is not obviously worth it. **Don't add a graduate program to this plan.**

### Two things this table says out loud

**1. The Dean → Director move is worth ~$28K and it's the highest-certainty financial gain in this entire plan.** It requires no capital, no risk, and no permission from anyone outside the charter. Do it regardless of what happens with the venture.

**2. There is a valley in year three, and nobody plans for it.** Combined income peaks in 2027-28 at roughly $137–157K, then *drops* to $110–125K when Tiffany goes full-time at a 15-student startup school. A small school genuinely cannot pay more than a good Director of Special Education role. The venture doesn't out-earn the ladder until roughly **year 4–5**.

**Three mitigations, all decided in year 2, not year 3:**
- **Bank the year-2 consulting fee.** That $25–45K is the bridge — treat it as reserve, not income.
- **Sign the Founder's Employment Agreement before the valley**, with a stated salary and a step-up schedule tied to enrollment.
- **Time the jump to the shallowest point** — going full-time in July 2028 rather than January means one summer of overlap income, not six months of neither.

> **One structural constraint worth knowing now:** if the school eventually pursues NYSED approval, the **Reimbursable Cost Manual caps executive compensation**. The non-approved path has no such cap. So the salary-maximizing route and the access-maximizing route diverge somewhat — Door A trades personal upside for scale, predictability, and reach. That's a values decision, not a spreadsheet one, and it belongs to Tiffany.

Five things, and none of them require a building:

1. **Three years of normed, published outcome data** — the only such dataset in this market.
2. **A working referral relationship with 3–5 special-education law firms.**
3. **A practice billing $200K+/year** with proven unit economics and 2–3 certified interventionists.
4. **Director of Special Education** on the résumé, with supervisory and budget experience.
5. **A funded decision about the school**, made with data instead of hope.

If the answer in August 2028 is *no school* — the practice is still a real, profitable business that pays Tiffany a second income and serves families nobody else serves. **That's what makes it an MVP: the downside is a good outcome.**
