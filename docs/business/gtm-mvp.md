# MVP go-to-market plan

This document covers MVP cohort recruitment, not growth. It supersedes the current-product sections of the historical [GTM plan](../gtm-plan.md) for this reading product; the old document is not edited or deleted.

## Objective

Recruit 30–50 families for a consented MVP cohort through the founder’s NYC teacher network, measure whether children return through week eight, and learn whether teachers will recommend a genuinely useful free core without being placed at ethical risk. The cohort is a seeding activity. It is not proof of a scalable acquisition engine, a paid conversion rate, or school demand.

Retention comes first. Distribution is the second unknown, after the product has enough content for a child to reach week eight. Revenue is third. A cohort recruited before the app has depth would only measure the existing four-week content cliff.

## Recruitment motion

The founder will prepare a concise teacher-facing explanation: who the child is, what the app teaches, what it does not claim, why it is free, how the child can use it alone, and how privacy works. A teacher may share that information with an appropriate family through ordinary professional judgment. The family chooses whether to participate. The product never requires a teacher to administer sessions, collect school data, or monitor a dashboard.

The target is 30–50 families, not a claim that this number is statistically representative. The cohort study requires a parent-signed consent artifact for aggregate weekly counters, consistent with ADR-000 D6. The cohort path uses a code, remains visible and revocable, uploads only aggregate counters under a random participant token, and has no name, account, or device identifier. Families outside the cohort use the fully on-device free product.

## Hard ethical constraint

No referral compensation of any kind is permitted. This includes any payment, business relationship, commission, revenue share, discount tied to the recommender, premium access tied to the recommendation, or other benefit. Chancellor’s Regulation C-110 II.D.2 bars any type of business relationship with a student at the teacher’s assigned school or their siblings. Georgia, Texas, and Massachusetts educator ethics codes independently bar the arrangement described in the research. The exposure lands on the teacher rather than the company, and the teachers are the channel.

The founder must not ask a teacher to cross that boundary for growth. A teacher recommendation is voluntary and informational. If a proposed campaign creates uncertainty about a teacher’s employment or ethics obligations, it does not run until counsel resolves it.

## The one written permission

The source research identifies one written permission worth using: a NYC teacher may advertise in publications of the PTA or parents’ organizations of other schools, under the COIB DOE FAQ. This permission is narrow. It does not authorize school sales, classroom use, student-roster access, or a commercial relationship with families at the teacher’s assigned school. Materials should use the free-core truth and direct families to the static safety/support pages.

## Reviews are the only available currency

Families who benefit may review the product publicly. Reviews are the only available currency for this channel. They must be voluntary, honest, and not purchased or conditioned on access. The team may invite feedback and make it easy to find the App Store review surface, but it may not manufacture social proof or imply that a teacher or family owes a favorable review.

## App Store and Kids Category positioning

The listing should position IEP & Thrive in the Kids Category if the legal and product gates are satisfied. The description must say that the child can open the app and receive systematic decoding instruction on a phone, free, without an account or adult beside them. It must not claim diagnosis, guaranteed reading outcomes, absence of alternatives, or a parent-held record before that feature exists.

The listing and screenshots must reflect the actual MVP: bundled instruction, short sessions, age-respectful agency, offline behavior, and free learning. They must not show a parent portal, subscription checkout, school dashboard, or export that is not shipped. Kids Category, COPPA, age-assurance, accessibility, and network-permission review are release gates, not marketing copy exercises.

## What is out of scope

District and school sales are out of scope for years. No district procurement, roster integration, SSO, DPA, classroom deployment, or NYC school sale is part of this motion. The growth timeline identifies an eight-to-eighteen-month procurement cycle and a conflict between school sales and the teacher network while the founder is NYCPS-employed. Enterprise discussion requires outcome data and a deliberate channel decision later.

Paid acquisition, influencer campaigns, child-directed advertising, third-party ads, and a national launch are also out of scope. The first result we need is a trustworthy retention curve from a small real cohort.

## Success and stop conditions

Recruitment succeeds operationally when 30–50 families enroll under the approved consent process and the aggregate measurement path produces a valid denominator and weekly counters. GTM succeeds strategically only if the product first clears the week-eight retention gate and the team gets an honest answer on teacher recommendation. If retention fails, the team fixes content, instruction, agency, or usability before expanding recruitment. If teachers do not recommend, the response is to understand the ethical and product reasons, not to create incentives that the channel cannot lawfully carry.
