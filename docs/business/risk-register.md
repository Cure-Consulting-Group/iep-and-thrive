# MVP risk register

Risks are ranked by their ability to invalidate the product thesis. Likelihood and impact are qualitative planning judgments; where the source does not provide a probability, the rating is an assumption to validate through the cohort or counsel. Owners must update the trigger status after each gate.

| Rank / ID | Description | Likelihood | Impact | Owner | Mitigation | Materialisation trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 / R-REV | The target families are defined by not having money, so the parents most likely to convert may be affluent families the product is explicitly not for. The paid parent record may not support the business. | High | Critical | Founder + finance owner | Keep instruction free; validate the record only after retention; offer $0 hardship access on request; investigate a philanthropic or grant leg without changing the product thesis; model 1%, 3%, and 5% conversion | Paid conversion materially trails the 3% planning case, hardship demand is substantial, or conversion clusters in the anti-persona |
| 2 / R-RET | Children may not return after the initial novelty, especially when content is thin, pacing is wrong, or the incumbent’s agency failures are reproduced. | High | Critical | Product + educator | Build the ~120-node floor; make narration skippable; infer placement; avoid remedial lectures; measure week-eight retention against roughly 7% monthly churn | Week-two continuation falls, week-eight retention fails the pre-registered gate, or sessions stop at the content cliff |
| 3 / R-DIST | Teacher recommendation without classroom use is unproven, and the founder’s network may not produce 30–50 families. | High | High | Founder | Treat the NYC network as cohort seeding only; use the narrow written PTA/other-school publication permission; make the free core genuinely useful; invite voluntary honest reviews | Fewer than the cohort target enroll, teachers decline to share, or recommendations require the company to operate through the teacher |
| 4 / R-GAP | The market gap may be empty for a reason. Lexia’s published 1024×768 floor may be a deliberate constraint from a better-resourced company, and several dyslexia phone apps already exist with almost no users. | Medium | Critical | Founder + product | Test the phone experience with real children; distinguish form-factor demand from instructional demand; do not infer demand from competitor absence | Children with the target profile do not start, cannot complete, or do not return despite adequate content |
| 5 / R-COMP | There is no solo unfunded comparable for a teacher-to-parent recommendation and parent-purchase model. Existing functioning loops had venture funding and a team. | High | High | Founder | Treat every revenue and distribution claim as a hypothesis; stage retention, distribution, then revenue; maintain explicit assumptions | The cohort reveals operating work, support, or content demands incompatible with solo delivery |
| 6 / R-CONT | Curriculum authoring is the long pole and needs an educator rather than faster code. The product can ship software without having enough valid instruction. | High | Critical | Educator/content owner | Reserve educator capacity; author against the taxonomy and template; validate decodability and prerequisites in CI; budget authoring separately | The 120-node floor misses freeze, examples use untaught graphemes, or audio and mastery rules are incomplete |
| 7 / R-CRASH | Removing Crashlytics removes the team’s own crash visibility. On a child-facing offline product, a crash may be experienced simply as the app not working. | Medium | High | Engineering | Use MetricKit, automated tests, release validation, support path, and device-matrix testing; keep the no-network invariant; prioritize crash reproduction | Crash-free evidence is unavailable, MetricKit indicates elevated failures, or families report unexplained exits that cannot be reproduced |
| 8 / R-LEGAL | The child-alone posture creates COPPA, age-assurance, Kids Category, voice, and stroke-data questions. | Medium | Critical | Counsel | Preserve D1 zero-network design; do not collect accounts or learner data in MVP; answer the four ADR-000 counsel questions before the record phase; complete required written policies | Counsel identifies a notice, consent, biometric, age, or Kids Category requirement that the MVP design cannot meet |
| 9 / R-MEASURE | The retention result may be untrustworthy if cohort consent, aggregate upload, denominator, or missing-data handling is unclear. | Medium | Critical | Study owner + engineering | Pre-register the retained definition; use the D6 code and random token; report missing uploads and exclusions; preserve calculation version | The team cannot state how many devices entered, returned in week eight, or were missing data |
| 10 / R-AFFORD | Annual payment may create a cash-flow barrier even when the annual price is lower than the comparison set. | Medium | High | Founder + finance owner | Keep $5.99 monthly option and no-questions $0 hardship tier; never paywall instruction; track plan mix and requests | Families complete the free learning path but abandon the record solely at annual checkout |
| 11 / R-DEVICE | A phone experience may fail on the oldest supported device through performance, screen, audio, or accessibility constraints. | Medium | High | Engineering + accessibility owner | Declare the support matrix; measure cold launch, frame rate, memory, audio, and WCAG 2.2 AA behavior before cohort release | The oldest supported device cannot sustain a 10–15-minute session or an accessible interaction path |
| 12 / R-DEPTH | The app reaches the current ~35 engine-backed levels and runs out before the study window. | High | High | Product + educator | Treat 120 as the minimum floor; use the 72–96 eight-week consumption arithmetic; gate content availability in CI and cohort review | A child has no eligible next level before week eight or content availability declines by cohort week |

## Risk operating rules

The first two gates are not marketing problems. Revenue risk is ranked first because the business model can fail even if the child product works; retention is second because no later phase matters without children returning. Distribution is third because ethics removes the usual incentive mechanic and no comparable answers the question. The empty-slot, solo-founder, curriculum, and crash risks are delivery and thesis risks, not reasons to conceal uncertainty.

The founder reviews this table at cohort kickoff, week two, week eight, and before any post-MVP record build. A trigger changes the decision log and mitigation owner. It does not authorize expanding scope, school sales, or relaxing the architecture.
