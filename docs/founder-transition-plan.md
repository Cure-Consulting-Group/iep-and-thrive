**For:** Tiffany Lozado, Dean · Rashad Cureton

**Companion:** `docs/master-plan.md`

---

## 1. The good news: you dodged the hard case

Had Tiffany been a NYC DOE employee, this strategy would be largely unbuildable while employed. NYC Charter Chapter 68 would bar it three ways — and the COIB publishes an example almost identical to our situation:

> **Q.** *I am a school psychologist. [A private firm] wishes to hire me to perform evaluations of children who attend DOE schools… my evaluations would be submitted and reviewed at Impartial Hearings… I might even have to appear at Impartial Hearings. May I perform this work?*
>
> **A. No, you may not.** *The Conflicts Law prohibits City employees from appearing, directly or indirectly, before any City agency, including the DOE. Your personal appearances at Impartial Hearings, and the submission and review of your evaluations, would be appearances before the DOE, which are prohibited by the Conflicts Law.*
> — [NYC COIB, *Questions Frequently Asked by DOE Employees*](https://www.nyc.gov/html/conflicts/downloads/pdf2/leaflets/doemailer.pdf)

That would have killed the testifying-witness thesis outright, and a **one-year post-employment bar** on appearing before DOE would have blocked another full year after departure.

**Charter school employees are generally not "public servants" under Chapter 68.** A NY charter school is operated by an independent not-for-profit education corporation under Education Law Article 56; its employees work for that corporation, not for a City agency. The impartial-hearing bar and the one-year post-employment bar do not attach.

> **[VERIFY]** Two edge cases break this: conversion charters, and charters where staff remain DOE employees by arrangement. Confirm Tiffany's actual employer of record on the paystub and in the employment agreement before relying on any of this.

## 2. What actually constrains Tiffany

Not statute. **Contract and relationship.**

| Constraint | Source | Bite |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-solicitation of families** | Employment agreement | **Sharpest risk.** ~19.3% of NYC charter enrollment has IEPs — Tiffany is embedded in the exact demographic. That proximity is an asset *and* the thing most likely to end badly |
| **Non-solicitation of staff** | Employment agreement | Don't recruit colleagues while employed. Full stop |
| **Outside activities / moonlighting approval** | Employee handbook, board policy | Usually permission-based, not prohibited. Get written approval |
| **Conflict-of-interest policy + annual disclosure** | N-PCL §715-a; Educ. Law §2854; charter bylaws | Every NY nonprofit must have one. **Disclose proactively and in writing** |
| **Confidentiality / student data** | FERPA, employment agreement | No student data, no roster, no records — ever, for any purpose |
| **School resources** | Handbook | No work email, no work devices, no work hours |
| **Non-compete** | Employment agreement | NY courts disfavor these for most employees and enforcement is narrow — but read it anyway |
| **Authorizer relationship** | NYSED Charter School Office / SUNY CSI / NYC DOE | If NYC DOE is the authorizer, testifying against DOE creates an *appearance* issue, not a legal one. Brief the charter's board rather than let them discover it |

### The bright lines — never cross these

1. **Never recruit a family from your own school.** Not a conversation, not a mention, not a referral through a third party. If a family from your school approaches you unprompted, refer them to counsel and recuse yourself entirely.
2. **Never recruit a colleague while employed.**
3. **Never use school time, email, devices, letterhead, or data.**
4. **Never use student records** — yours or anyone's — for program design, market sizing, or recruitment.
5. **Disclose in writing, early.** A surprised board is a hostile board. A board told in advance, in writing, with a recusal plan, is usually fine.

### The things Tiffany *can* do — which is nearly everything

Design curriculum and the assessment framework · make the SEQA and Rate Setting Unit calls · meet special-education attorneys · take funder meetings · take DOE contracting meetings · speak publicly about SPED practice · **testify at impartial hearings** · serve in a founding role at the nonprofit (with disclosure) · accept consulting compensation (with written outside-employment approval).

Compared to the DOE branch, essentially the entire strategy is available.

### Phase 2 — Jan–Jun 2027 + summer (~290 hrs + summer)

Funder meetings for the guarantee pool (~40) · DOE contracting conversations (~40) · curriculum and IEP-goal assessment framework (~100) · charter petition support (~20) · facility visits (~20, mostly Rashad) · **summer 2027 cohort delivered** (already Tiffany's job, and it doubles as building the model).

**Fits, tightly.** Summers are the release valve.

### Phase 3 — Sept 2027 onward: **this is where it breaks**

Recruitment cannot be delegated. Families choosing a school for a child with an IEP want to meet the head of school — **Tiffany's credibility *is* the pitch.** School tours, attorney meetings, parent information sessions, admissions decisions, and hiring lead teachers all land in the same window, and it is the same window as a charter Dean's most demanding season.

**The incompatibility point is roughly September 2027 — about twelve months from now — not September 2028.**

## 4. Three ways to buy runway

**Option A — Hire a Director of Admissions & Family Partnership in mid-2027. *(Recommended.)***
Cost ~$120–150K loaded. They carry logistics, tours, follow-up, CRM, and the attorney-referral pipeline; Tiffany appears for the high-leverage moments — attorney meetings, funder meetings, and the final conversation with each family. **This buys a full additional year and costs less than a year's delay.** Honest caveat: Tiffany still needs 2–3 evenings or weekend days a week through the **Nov 2027 – Feb 2028** peak. That is survivable for one season alongside a demanding job. Barely.

**Option B — Reduced schedule or unpaid leave for the 2027-28 charter year.** Many schools permit it, it preserves the relationship, and it de-risks the jump without a hard resignation. **Ask early** — this is a budget-cycle decision for the charter, not a favor.

**Option C — Resign June 2027.** Cleanest and fastest, and the only path that makes a September 2027 opening realistic. Also the highest personal risk, since it precedes the guarantee pool closing.

### Recommended: A + B

Hire the admissions lead in mid-2027, request reduced schedule or leave for 2027-28, and **go full-time by July 2028** for a September 2028 opening. That gives Tiffany roughly **22 months of runway** and keeps the base-case timeline intact.

## 5. The compensation problem — solve it now, not later

Tiffany would work ~12 hrs/week for ~22 months and then leave a stable job for a startup school. **Nonprofits have no equity**, so there is nothing to vest. This must be structured up front or it quietly becomes a reason not to proceed.

**Three instruments, in order of preference:**

1. **Consulting fee funded by Mechanism 1 revenue, starting 2027.** DOE contracting revenue arrives before the school opens. Paying Tiffany a real consulting fee out of it compensates the pre-launch work *and* proves the model. Requires written outside-employment approval from the charter — usually granted.
2. **A Founder's Employment Agreement signed now.** Guarantees the Head of School role, a stated salary, and a start date certain. This is the thing that makes leaving a job rational rather than a leap.
3. **Deferred compensation for pre-launch work**, payable on a revenue threshold. Must satisfy IRS reasonable-compensation rules, and — if NYSED approval follows — the Reimbursable Cost Manual's compensation and related-party limits. **[VERIFY with counsel]**

### One governance point that matters more than it looks

**If Tiffany will become the paid Head of School, Tiffany should not be a voting trustee of the nonprofit.** Under N-PCL §715 and §715-a, a board member who is also a compensated employee triggers related-party scrutiny and must recuse from every compensation decision. It is cleaner, and reads better to funders, regulators, and a future NYSED reviewer, if Tiffany is **"Founder and Head of School designate"** while Rashad and independent trustees constitute the board.
