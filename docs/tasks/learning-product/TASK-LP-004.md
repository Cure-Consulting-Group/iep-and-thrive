## Problem and intended outcome

Local relationships can produce discovery interviews, but NYCPS classroom adoption requires policy and purchasing qualification. The current 2026–27 guidance changes the assumptions for younger learners and generative AI.

## Implementation scope

1. Create separate channel plans for direct families, independent tutors, private/charter schools, and NYCPS; identify buyer, authorization, device, budget cycle, and data-controller responsibilities for each.
2. Review the official sources in the audit source register with qualified school/privacy contacts; document current screen-use, AI, procurement, and data agreements before promising a classroom use case.
3. Design teacher-led and printable companion options for evaluation; do not assume that targeting children with IEPs creates an assistive-technology exemption.
4. Prepare a vendor evidence checklist: security architecture, subprocessors, retention/deletion, accessibility, instructional evidence, insurance/contracts where requested, support, and incident contact.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Given** a school requests a pilot, **when** the channel is qualified, **then** required institutional authorization and data agreements are identified before student data is used.

- **Given** policy changes, **when** the channel plan is reviewed, **then** the applicable product scope and claim language are updated.

## Validation and evidence

Dated policy matrix with primary-source links, interview evidence, and a documented go/no-go per channel. This is readiness work, not a guarantee of procurement approval.

## Rollout, migration, and recovery

No school outreach messages or applications are sent by this documentation ticket. Recheck policy at pilot and purchase time.
