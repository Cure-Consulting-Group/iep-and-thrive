## Problem and intended outcome

The school strategy, native game PRD, and tutoring roadmap point toward different businesses. Establish one working product brief: independently useful reading practice supported by the educator’s expertise, with tutoring retained as a distinct offer.

## Implementation scope

1. Interview the educator about the strongest initial instructional problem; specify prerequisite skills, grade/age range, exclusions, adult involvement, and intended setting. Treat grades 3–5 comprehension as a hypothesis, not an approved audience.
2. Specify the child, buyer, practitioner, and payer separately; map discovery through independent practice, adult review, renewal, and exit.
3. Define one reading quest and adult evidence view as the validation release. Defer school formation/facilities, broad math expansion, open-ended child chat, and district-wide platform work.
4. Produce PRD-LP-001 and a decision log that supersedes conflicting implementation priorities without deleting historical research.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Given** the team receives a new feature request, **when** it is evaluated, **then** the brief identifies whether it contributes to the chosen learner outcome and release gate.

- **Given** a family buys no tutoring, **when** the product journey is reviewed, **then** the proposed learning and support experience still works independently.

## Validation and evidence

Educator and product-owner review of the brief; trace every proposed launch capability to a learner need and measurable outcome. Record unresolved questions and their decision owners.

## Rollout, migration, and recovery

Documentation decision only. Do not change public promises or remove service access until the reviewed brief is approved.
