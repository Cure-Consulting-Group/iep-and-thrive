## What the scan can and cannot establish

It can list what exists. It cannot establish who wrote it, whether we hold rights to
distribute it, or whether an educator has approved it for instructional use. Those are
the three things TASK-LP-002 needs, and all three are currently **undocumented for every
asset**.

Two findings worth acting on:

1. **Git authorship is unusable as provenance.** Every asset's first commit is authored by
   `Your Name` — an unconfigured git identity. There is no machine-readable record of who
   wrote any of this. Attribution must come from the author directly, not from history.
2. **Orton-Gillingham is referenced in the curriculum.** OG is a named instructional
   approach with trademark and practitioner-certification considerations. Describing
   material as OG-based, or marketing it that way, is a claim that needs to match actual
   training and permitted usage. The audit already flagged unverified credential claims;
   this is where the instructional side of that sits.

## Required before any of this publishes

- [ ] Named instructional reviewer with allocated time and a substitute path.
- [ ] Author and rights recorded per asset, from the author — not inferred.
- [ ] Third-party passages, images and audio identified and licensed, or replaced.
- [ ] Informal probes separated from licensed or normed instruments. An informal check
      described as an assessment is a claim about measurement that will not survive review.
- [ ] Release checklist covering instructional accuracy, accessibility, cultural
      relevance, permission, and assessment separation.

Provenance and approval are the founder's and educator's to supply. Engineering can hold
the register and enforce draft status; it cannot manufacture the underlying facts.
