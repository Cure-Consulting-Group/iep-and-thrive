# Product vision

**September 6, 2026.** The north star. Everything in PRD-LP-001 and the roadmap serves this
document; where they conflict, this wins.

---

## The observation

Children with learning differences already have the screen.

They have more screen time than their peers, not less. What they do not have is anything on that
screen that teaches them to read. The apps built for struggling readers run on iPads in
classrooms or on desktops with a parent sitting alongside. The apps on the phone in the child's
hand are entertainment, or they are phonics products designed for five-year-olds and insulting to
a nine-year-old who already knows they are behind.

**The device is not the problem. The content on it is.**

## The vision

> **Every child who struggles to read can open their phone and be taught — properly, for free,
> by themselves.**

Not practice. Not games that reinforce what a teacher covered. Taught: introduced to a sound,
shown how it is written, guided through it, and moved forward when they have it.

## Why this is ours to build

Three facts, and the product sits where they intersect.

**The system rations.** ~34% of US public schools report being understaffed in special education.
In New York City, bilingual SETSS reached only 35% of the students entitled to it. A child waits
months for an evaluation, then waits again for a service that may not be staffed. The instruction
is legally mandated and practically unavailable.

**The alternatives assume money and an adult.** Lexia Core5 and Nessy both teach properly and
both sell direct to parents — at $175 and roughly $186 a year, on iPads and desktops, with
Lexia's own home reseller telling the parent *"The Parent is the Teacher."* That works for a
family with a spare device, a spare hour every evening, and the money. That is not most families,
and it is emphatically not the families the system has already failed.

**The incumbent is disliked by the children using it.** Lexia Core5 holds 3.51 stars across
14,209 ratings, against 4.7–4.8 for its peer set. Every recurring complaint is about pacing and
agency — narration that cannot be skipped, remedial lectures triggered by two wrong answers,
children "counting down the minutes until they can log off." Nobody says the teaching is wrong.
They say the child is trapped inside it.

A child who is already behind and already frustrated will not tolerate being trapped. That is a
product failure, and it is the one we are best placed to fix.

## What we believe

**Instruction is the free part.** A child's ability to learn to read is not a premium feature.
The full decoding sequence is free forever, for everyone, with no account and no gate. We sell
the parent a record of what their child learned — never the learning itself.

**The child is the user.** Not the parent, not the teacher, not the school. The design question
is always what a nine-year-old alone with a phone will actually do, and every adult-facing
feature is secondary to that.

**Rigor and delight are not in tension — the incumbent just failed at one of them.** Orton-
Gillingham is not the reason children dislike Lexia. Unskippable narration is. We keep the
sequence and fix the experience.

**Friction is the enemy of the mission.** Every gate between download and learning excludes
someone, and it excludes the least-resourced family first. No school, no evaluation, no
diagnosis, no subscription, no account, no adult required to begin.

**Evidence belongs to the parent.** The record we produce is theirs — to read, to export, to
bring to a CSE meeting if they want. Not ours, and not the school's.

## What we will not do

- **Sell to districts.** Not because it is beneath us, but because an eight-to-eighteen-month
  procurement cycle serves nobody waiting today, and building for it would bend the product
  toward a buyer who is not the child.
- **Charge for instruction**, ever, in any tier, for any skill.
- **Advertise to children.** No third-party ads, no sponsored content, no data sold. The amended
  COPPA Rule makes this a legal hazard; it is also simply not something we will do.
- **Require an adult.** A product that only works when a parent sits down with the child has
  reproduced the constraint we exist to remove.
- **Claim to diagnose.** We teach. We do not identify dyslexia and we do not replace an
  evaluation.
- **Pay for referrals.** Teachers may recommend and families may review. Nobody is compensated —
  it is barred by educator ethics rules in several states and it would corrupt the only
  distribution we have.

## How we will know it worked

Not downloads. Not ratings. Not revenue, first.

**A child who could not decode CVC words in January reads decodable text in June, and their
parent has the record to prove it.**

Everything else is instrumentation.
