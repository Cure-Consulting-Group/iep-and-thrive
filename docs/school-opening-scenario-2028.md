# SCENARIO: Opening the School, September 2028 — 16 Students, Direct-Payment Funded

**Date:** 2026-08-29
**For:** Tiffany Lozado, Dean · Rashad Cureton
**Question:** What does it take to stand up a 16-student school for fall 2028 on tuition reimbursement — what does it cost to run, and what does it pay?
**Companions:** `docs/master-plan.md` · `docs/market-research-002-carter-day-school.md` · `docs/two-year-mvp-roadmap.md` · `docs/legal/nys-sped-school-regulatory-brief.md`

## 1. What this scenario actually is

A **Door D school opened at the Door A headcount.**

Funding is IDEA unilateral placement — Carter reimbursement and *Connors*-type direct payment. No NYSED approval, no regional-need survey, no rate-setting cap, no ban on parent payment. Opening at **16 rather than 12** is a deliberate choice with two payoffs:

1. It pre-satisfies the **§200.7(c)(3) 16-student floor**, so a later Door A application shows NYSED a running school instead of a business plan.
2. It fills three classes of six almost completely, which is where the economics turn.

This is the master plan's **base case** — Sept 2028, ~24 months out. It is not the aggressive Sept 2027 case, which was rejected in favor of letting Tiffany take the Director of Special Education role first.

---

## 2. The headline finding

> **At 2028 cost levels, 16 students does not break even. Eighteen does.**

Two years of salary inflation in a special-education teacher shortage market outrun tuition escalation. Breakeven moves from ~15 students (2027 cost basis) to **~18 students (2028 cost basis)** in the base case, ~17 in the lean case.

Since three classes of six is **18 seats**, the school must run essentially full in year one. There is no slack.

**Students 17 and 18 cost almost nothing to serve and net ~$74K each.** Filling those two seats is the difference between a ~$107K loss and a ~$47K surplus. **Recruit to 18. Budget at 16.**

| 2030-31 | Head of School — 42 students | — | $160K | $160K |
| 2031-32 | Head of School — ~60 students | — | $180K | $180K |

**The valley is one year deep and $12–32K wide.**

That is materially better than the two-year roadmap's original framing, which had it at up to $47K. The reason is enrollment: **opening at 16 rather than 12–15 pays for a $125K head of school instead of a $110K one.** The four extra students fund the difference in the founder's own salary.

**Three mitigations, all decided in 2027-28 — not 2028-29:**

1. **Bank the 2027-28 consulting fee.** $25–45K set aside as reserve more than covers a $12–32K gap. Treat it as capital, not income.
2. **Sign the Founder's Employment Agreement before the valley**, with a stated salary and a step-up schedule tied to enrollment thresholds — not to time.
3. **Time the jump to July 2028.** Going full-time in July rather than January means one summer of overlap income instead of six months of neither.

### Rashad / Cure Consulting Group

| Year | Basis | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| 2028-29 | No capacity at 16 students | **$0** |
| 2029-30 | Management fee, 28 students | $55,000 |
| 2030-31 | Management fee, 42 students | $85,000 |

Door D imposes **no RCM 35% non-direct-cost cap**, so a management fee to CCG is legally clean while the school is unapproved.

> **⚠ Design for this now.** If the school later pursues Door A approval, that fee comes inside the **35% non-direct-cost parameter** and under RCM related-party and executive-compensation rules. Structure the CCG relationship at formation so it survives that transition rather than having to be unwound. **[VERIFY with counsel.]**

### Working capital — the number that actually decides this

Capital need is a function of **cohort mix, not enrollment.** Tier definitions per `strategy-nyc-alignment-and-financing.md` §III Layer 2.

| Cohort mix | Receivable carried | Cash needed | Or, as first-loss guarantee |
|---|---|---|---|
| **16 Tier A** — pendency continuers, already funded | ~$0 | **~$450K** | — |
| **8 A / 6 B / 2 C** — mixed | ~$655K | ~$1.05M | **~$525K** |
| **16 Tier C** — $0-down, unrepresented | $1,312,000 | ~$1.6M | **~$800K** |

A philanthropic first-loss guarantee is roughly **2× more capital-efficient than cash** — at an 85%+ win rate it is rarely drawn. **Cap Tier C seats at exactly what the guarantee covers.** That discipline is what keeps the mission from killing the school.

**Planning number: ~$450–525K to open Tier-A-heavy, of which roughly half can be a guarantee rather than deployed cash.**

## 8. Why 2028 beats 2027 — and what it costs

### The case for waiting

1. **Three years of outcome data** instead of two, feeding both prong-two hearings and the eventual regional-need argument.
2. **The Director of Special Education title**, with supervisory and budget ownership — exactly the record a §200.7 fiscal reviewer and any future Educational Director qualification will look for.
3. **The $25–45K consulting fee banked** as the reserve that covers the valley.
4. **Two full admissions cycles** to build the attorney referral network. MARKET-002 puts that at 2–3 years; the 2027 path compressed it to about four months.
5. **Real time** for the four validation calls, the guarantee pool, and the facility search.
6. **Mechanism 1 revenue** — DOE contracting — can start in 2027 and run through, offsetting pre-opening cost.

### The honest cost of waiting

**A year of political risk on a revenue stream the city is actively trying to shrink.** The Mamdani administration has publicly booked **$147–149M in avoided due-process litigation savings**; City Journal and the Manhattan Institute are both pushing for caps; FY26 budgeted $935M against $1.3B actually spent. Every year of delay is a year in which the Carter door narrows.

**The mitigation is the strategy itself** — price below the settlement average, publish outcome data no incumbent publishes, and serve the segment the equity critique says is excluded. Be the cheaper, measurable, more equitable option *before* the reform arrives.

## 10. The honest summary

**This is a breakeven school in year one that becomes a real business in year two.**

Nobody gets rich in 2028-29. Tiffany takes a one-year, $12–32K pay cut that a banked consulting fee covers. Rashad takes nothing. The school runs a small loss at 16 and a small surplus at 18, and the difference between those two outcomes is two families.

**What makes it worth doing** is what happens after: +$163K in year two, +$531K in year three, a founder salary that clears her Director peak by 2029-30, and — the actual prize — a running 16-student school with three years of published outcome data walking into a NYSED regional-need conversation that no applicant has ever framed the way we intend to frame it.

**What makes it risky** is enrollment concentration. At 18 seats, two empty ones cost $148K. Everything in year one depends on filling three classes from a referral network that has to be built between now and January 2028.

**The single highest-leverage action available today is unchanged from the master plan:** lock the summer 2026 outcome data and track every final report through September CSE season. It is free, it expires, and every number in this document depends on it.
