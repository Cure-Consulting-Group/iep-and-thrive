## Part I — The week

### The constraint that actually matters isn't hours, it's business hours

A 4:00 PM finish gives roughly 12–13 weekday hours before 7:00 PM. That's enough. The real scarcity is **daytime hours**, because the highest-value counterparties keep government schedules:

| Counterparty | When they're reachable | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| SEQA regional offices, NYSED Rate Setting Unit, Nondistrict Unit | 9–5, often gone by 4:45 | **Scarce — must be protected** |
| DOE Office of Special Education, district administrators | 9–5 | **Scarce** |
| Foundations and funders | 9–5, but schedulable weeks out | Manageable |
| Special-education attorneys | Frequently available 5–7 PM | **Free — attorneys work late** |
| Parents and families | Evenings and weekends are *better* | **Free — this is when they want you** |
| Program design, curriculum, data, writing | Async | **Free** |

**Design principle:** buy a small number of daytime slots deliberately, and push everything else after 4:30.

### The Agency Day

Batch every business-hours call into **one personal or comp day per month**. Six to eight government and funder calls in a single day is more effective than one a week anyway — you carry context between them, and you can escalate the same afternoon.

Between Agency Days, use the **12:00–1:00 lunch window** for anything short.

### Weekly template

| | Block | Focus | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | 4:30–6:30 | **Data & documentation** — outcome records, progress reports, assessment scoring | Start the week on the asset nobody else has |
| **Tue** | 4:30–6:30 | **Relationships** — attorney calls, parent conversations, funder follow-up | Attorneys and parents are *most* available now |
| **Wed** | 4:30–6:30 | **Program build** — curriculum, assessment framework, IEP-goal mapping | Mid-week, highest cognitive load |
| **Thu** | 4:30–5:30 | **Rashad sync** — standing 60 minutes, agenda-driven, decisions only | One meeting a week; everything else async |
| **Thu** | 5:30–6:30 | Overflow / correspondence | Absorbs the week's spillover |
| **Fri** | — | **Off** | Non-negotiable |
| **Sat** | — | **Off** | Non-negotiable |
| **Sun** | 9:00–1:00 | **Deep work** — the program design document, application narrative, long-form writing | The only 4-hour uninterrupted block in the week |
| *Monthly* | One weekday | **Agency Day** — batch all business-hours calls | Buys the scarce resource |

**≈ 12 hrs/week + 8 hrs/month.** Friday and Saturday are protected. Over 22 months, burnout is a more likely failure mode than any regulatory obstacle — treat the days off as part of the plan, not slack in it.

## Part II — The program, year by year

The organizing idea: **each year's program is designed to produce the evidence and relationships the next year's funding mechanism requires.** The program is not just delivery. It is the instrument that unlocks the next payer.

### The payer progression

```
Parents  →  Vouchers & contracts  →  Placement funding  →  Rate-set approval
(2026-27)     (2027-28)                (2028-30)             (2030+)
```

Each step is a different payer, and each requires a different credential. You cannot skip one.

---

### Summer 2027 — *Prove and instrument*

**Funding:** still 100% parent-pay.
**The change:** restructure to the ESY specification even though you cannot bill it yet.

| | Summer 2026 (delivered) | Summer 2027 (target) |
|---|---|---|
| Days | 24 (Mon–Thu × 6 wks) | **30 (Mon–Fri × 6 wks)** |
| Hours/day | 4 | **5** |
| Total instruction | 96 hrs | **150 hrs** |
| Assessment | Internal pre/post | **Normed instrument + goal-attainment scaling against each child's actual IEP goals** |

**Why do this a year before you can bill it:**
1. It is the format Education Law §4408 requires — 30 days, five days/week, ≥5 hours for K–6. Building it now means you're compliant the day approval lands.
2. **It makes the outcome data legible to the people who matter.** "We run a 30-day, 5-hour ESY-format program with normed pre/post data" is a fundamentally different claim to an attorney, a CSE chair, or a hearing officer than "six weeks, four days, four hours."
3. Normed assessment is what converts internal rubrics into evidence. Goal-attainment scaling against real IEP goals is what wins prong two.

**Pricing implication, stated honestly:** 150 hours vs. 96 is a 56% increase in instruction and staffing. A $3,500–4,000 price does not survive it. Expect **$5,500–6,500**, with a smaller cohort. That will lose some current families. It is still the right call, because the summer program's job has changed from *revenue* to *evidence*.

> ### ⚠️ The hard call: Tiffany stops teaching
> Summer is simultaneously the only large building window and the window the program runs in. 150 hours of delivery would consume it entirely.
>
> **From summer 2027, Tiffany is Program Director, not lead teacher.** Hire 2–3 certified interventionists to deliver; Tiffany supervises, runs the assessment, and does the parent debriefs — keeping roughly half of summer hours for building.
>
> This is uncomfortable because the teaching *is* the product quality. It is also non-negotiable: it's what makes the model scalable, and 8 NYCRR §200.20(b)(5) will eventually require an **Educational Director**, not a teacher who also administrates.

### School Year 2027-28 — *Get paid by someone other than parents*

**The new product: an after-school and Saturday IEP-aligned intervention program.**

This is the bridge, and it does four things at once:

1. **Keeps families year-round** instead of losing them every September.
2. **Generates longitudinal outcome data.** A six-week summer cannot demonstrate durable gains. A 30-week school year can — and durability is what a hearing officer, a funder, and a NYSED reviewer all actually want to see.
3. **Creates the service line you can contract to DOE** (Mechanism 1).
4. **Is compatible with a 4:00 PM finish — but only because Tiffany doesn't deliver it.** She designs, supervises, and reviews the data. Hired interventionists teach. Same structure as the summer.

**How it gets publicly funded:**

> **[VERIFY — high priority]** In NYC, when DOE cannot provide a student's mandated **SETSS** (Special Education Teacher Support Services), families may receive a voucher to hire an independent, NYS-certified special-education teacher, with DOE paying the provider. Related services follow a parallel **RSA / P4** path. If that mechanism operates as described, **SETSS vouchers are the single best on-ramp in this entire plan** — academic instruction, IEP-mandated, DOE-paid, requiring exactly Tiffany's credential, in chronic shortage.
>
> **Confirm current SETSS/P3 provider rules, rates, and eligibility directly with the DOE Office of Special Education before building around it.** These rules changed recently and I have not verified the current state.

The Comptroller's *Course Correction* independently recommends improving the voucher system and raising DOE rates, and the FY26 budget added **$30M+ for contracted-out services** — so the direction of travel supports this, whatever the current mechanics.

**Revenue mix target for 2027-28:** ~60% parent-pay, ~40% voucher/contract.

### Summer 2028 — *The admissions funnel*

The summer program's job changes again. It becomes:
- **The pipeline.** Families who attend summer 2028 are the September 2028 enrollment list. Nobody enrolls a child with an IEP in an unproven first-year school sight unseen — but they will enroll after a good summer.
- **Staff onboarding.** The teachers hired for the school run the summer as their trial and their training.
- **The final dataset** before the school opens.

---

### September 2028 — *The school opens*

**Open with grades 2–4, three groups of 5–6, targeting 15–18 students.**

Three reasons this specific configuration:

1. **It pre-clears the 16-student minimum.** 8 NYCRR §200.7(c)(3) requires 16 students for approved programs. Opening at 16+ means that barrier is already satisfied when you file in 2029 — you never have to grow into it under scrutiny.
2. **Grades 2–4 is the identification window.** Dyslexia surfaces, the gap becomes visible, and parents move. It is also where intervention produces the strongest published outcomes.
3. **Skip kindergarten.** A K student has no data history, which makes a FAPE-denial claim much harder to prove.

Winston Prep LI starts at grade 3, leaving grades 1–2 uncontested on the low end.

---

### Growth — add one grade a year and backfill

| Year | Grades | Students | Primary funding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2028-29 | 2–4 | **15–18** | Direct payment (~70%), parent-pay (~30%) |
| 2029-30 | 2–5 | **~28** | Direct payment (~90%) |
| 2030-31 | 1–6 | **~42** | Rate-set approval begins |
| 2031-32 | 1–8 | **~60** | ~100% public, rate-set + P-1 |

Standard new-school mechanic: never open the full grade band at once. Add a grade above, backfill below, let the culture and the data compound.

**The summer program never stops.** From 2028 it serves double duty — ESY for enrolled students (billable once approved) and the admissions funnel for external families.
