## 1. Where this leaves us

Three memos in, the position is clear and it is not the one we started with.

**What we thought we were building:** a Long Island summer intensive that grows into a Long Island school, priced at $3,500–$4,000, starting with one cohort of six, eventually taking public money.

**What the research says we are actually building:** a **New York City special-education school**, funded first by direct-payment placements and ultimately by NYSED approval, operated by a **501(c)(3) with a Regents charter**, whose defensible asset is **outcome data nobody else in the market produces** and whose founder's credibility as a testifying Dean is the product.

**Four things are now settled:**

1. **The summer program is not the business — it is the evidence engine.** Keep running it. It generates the outcome data, the parent pipeline, and the proof of practice that every subsequent step depends on.
2. **Long Island is the wrong center of gravity.** 92–98% of New York's due-process filings are NYC. The rest of the state filed 358 and falling. Our experience and target demographic are in the city; the market is in the city; the school should serve the city.
3. **Corporate structure is the gating decision.** Nonprofit + Regents charter unlocks the financing instruments *and* is required for NYSED approval. Nearly everything else waits on it.
4. **The endgame is approval, not litigation.** Carter is the bridge. Approval is where the financing problem for non-affluent families disappears permanently, because the city pays directly.

**What is still genuinely open** — and should not be papered over:

- Whether SEQA will accept documented Carter volume as regional-need evidence. **Nobody has asked.** One phone call.
- Actual NYC 853 day-school rates. The entire savings argument to DOE rests on this number and we do not have it. **One phone call.**
- Whether a Nassau or outer-borough site can pull Brooklyn and Manhattan families. **Ten phone calls.**
- Whether an approved school may simultaneously serve unilaterally-placed families. **Counsel.**

Four unknowns, three of them answerable in a week.

## 2. The asset we are sitting on right now, and the window that is closing

The inaugural cohort ran **July 7 – August 14, 2026**. It ended nine days ago.

Per the Year 1 OKRs, that cohort was instrumented for exactly the thing this entire strategy needs:

| OKR | Target | Why it matters now |
|---|---|---|
| O2/KR1 — students with documented IEP-goal progress via pre/post assessment | 100% | **This is Data Point #1 of the outcome record no competitor publishes** |
| O2/KR2 — parents who submit the final report to CSE | 80%+, measured in September | **Direct evidence of district engagement — and the seed of regional-need documentation** |
| O2/KR3 — weekly reports delivered on time | 100% | Proof of the progress-monitoring discipline that wins prong two |
| O1/KR4 — parent NPS | 70+ | Referral engine and testimony |

**September CSE season starts in weeks.** Every one of those final reports is about to land on a district table. That is the single most time-sensitive thing in this document, and it is free.

**Do this now, before anything else in this plan:**

1. **Lock the pre/post data into a defensible format** — baseline, intervention, post, per IEP goal, per student. Not a marketing summary. An evidentiary record.
2. **Track what happens to every final report in September.** Which CSE received it, whether it changed anything, what the district said. That is regional-need evidence and prong-two evidence simultaneously.
3. **Get written parent consent now** to use de-identified outcome data publicly. Retrofitting consent later is painful.
4. **Publish the cohort's aggregate results.** First school in this market to do it. Costs nothing.

If nothing else in this plan happens for six months, do these four things.

## 3. What the iOS app becomes

Sprint 6 shipped the core learning loop; Sprint 7 has the test harness merged and Firebase sync in flight. In the old plan this was a product. In the new plan it is something more useful:

**The iOS app is the outcome-reporting engine.**

It already captures per-student, per-session data with a Firebase sync path: mission completion, level progression, `LetterTracer` accuracy and coverage at production thresholds, Snap Cubes target-count correctness. That is objective, timestamped, per-skill performance data — which is precisely what a Pay-for-Success contract, an impartial hearing record, and a published outcomes report all require, and precisely what no incumbent school has.

**What it needs to become that** is not more features. It is a mapping layer: tie each level and target to an IEP goal domain, add a structured pre/post assessment mode, and generate the report. The bones are built; the instrumentation is the gap.

**Reprioritize accordingly.** Design assets and telemetry tuning matter less than IEP-goal mapping and report generation. The app stops being a side quest and becomes the moat.

## 4. Timeline

Four hard gates set the clock, and only one of them is under our control:

| Gate | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Private-school admissions cycle** | Sept → March, annually | **The master clock.** Families choose a school for the following September between fall and early spring. Miss the window, lose a full year |
| **Facility certified for school use** | 9–18 months | Certificate of occupancy for educational use, fire safety inspection, accessibility. Longer in NYC. **The long pole** |
| **Regents provisional charter** | 6–12 months *(estimate)* | Petition → Office of Counsel → program offices → Board of Regents agenda. Regents meet roughly monthly. **[VERIFY]** — no published SLA found |
| **NYSED approval** | 24–30 months from regional-need filing | Regional need 3–6 mo, application assembly 3–6 mo, review 60 days after *complete*, then conditional approval up to one school year with ≥2 site visits before final |

### Base case

| Window | Phase | What happens | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aug–Dec 2026** | **Harvest & decide** | Lock summer outcome data; track September CSE outcomes; make the 4 validation calls; **form the 501(c)(3)**; file the Regents petition; begin DOE provider onboarding | Summer 2026 (banked) |
| **Jan–Jun 2027** | **First public revenue** | DOE contracting begins — RSA/P4 voucher work is a far faster on-ramp than a prime contract; facility search; guarantee-pool fundraising; charter lands mid-2027 | **Contract revenue starts** |
| **Jul–Aug 2027** | **Cohort 2** | Second summer intensive — two years of outcome data | Summer 2027 |
| **Sept 2027–Jan 2028** | **Build** | Facility secured and certified; head of school and lead teachers hired; enrollment contract drafted; **recruitment opens for Sept 2028** | Contract revenue |
| **Sept 2028** | **🎓 School opens** | 12–16 students, Tier-A heavy (pendency continuers), direct-payment structure, priced below the settlement average, publishing outcomes from day one | **~$0.8M** |
| **Spring 2029** | **File for approval** | Regional need argued from the city's Carter budget **plus three years of our own outcome data** | ~$0.8M |
| **2029–30** | **Conditional approval year** | Two NYSED site visits, CFR and governance training | ~$1.5–2M |
| **Sept 2030** | **✅ Final approval** | P-1 / Nickerson eligibility. DOE routes families to us. **Financing problem dissolves** | ~$2.9M+ |

**Headline: ~9 months to first public revenue. ~25 months to open the school. ~4 years to the endgame.**

### Aggressive case — school opens September 2027 (~13 months)

Possible, but only if **all** of these hold:
- A **turnkey facility already certified for educational use** — a sublease inside a church, an existing school, or a community center. No build-out, no use-group change.
- Recruitment opens by **January 2027**.
- The first cohort is **almost entirely Tier A pendency continuers**, who can move mid-cycle without a new appropriateness fight.
- The guarantee pool is committed by December 2026.

Realistic risk: a half-full first cohort and a thin evidentiary record going into the approval application. **Only take this path if a turnkey site appears.** Do not force it.

### Conservative case — September 2029

If the charter runs long, the facility search stalls, or the guarantee pool doesn't close, this slips a year cleanly. That is survivable because Mechanism 1 revenue and the summer program continue throughout. **The plan degrades gracefully, which is the main argument for sequencing it this way.**

## 5. What it costs to open

| Item | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Entity, charter petition, counsel, enrollment contract | $25–50K | Regents provisional charter fee is nominal ($100); the cost is legal time |
| Facility deposit, build-out, certification | $150–500K | **The widest variable.** A turnkey sublease collapses this to the low end |
| Pre-opening staffing — head of school, admissions, 6 months | ~$150K | Cannot recruit families without a head of school in place |
| Year 1 operating loss at 12 students | ~$92K | Breakeven is ~14 students |
| **Working capital / guarantee pool** | **$500K guarantee** *(or ~$940K cash)* | A first-loss guarantee is 2× more capital-efficient than cash. **Structure it as a guarantee** |
| **Total to open** | **~$800K–$1.5M** | Of which roughly $500K can be a philanthropic guarantee rather than deployed cash |

Mechanism 1 revenue and the summer program offset part of this from 2027 onward.

## 7. The honest summary

This is a materially larger undertaking than the summer program. It needs roughly a million dollars, a nonprofit conversion, a facility, a multi-year regulatory campaign, and a founder willing to be the public face and the testifying witness for a decade. That is a real go/no-go, and it belongs to Tiffany and Rashad, not to a research memo.

**What makes it worth considering** is that the pieces line up unusually well. The market is a billion dollars and publicly documented. The city has promised savings it does not yet know how to produce. The Comptroller has published the specification. The incumbents have two open flanks — no access for non-wealthy families, no outcome data at all — and neither is expensive to take. The founder's credentials are the exact asset the legal test rewards. And the summer program that already exists is the evidence engine the whole thing runs on.

**What makes it risky** is that it depends on a revenue stream the city is actively trying to shrink, in a political environment where both the left and the right want it smaller. The mitigation is the strategy itself: be the cheaper, measurable, more equitable option, and be that *before* the reform arrives rather than after.

**Start with the four phone calls and the September data.** Everything after that is a decision you will be better equipped to make in ninety days than today.
