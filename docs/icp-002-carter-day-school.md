# Ideal Customer Profile — Carter-Funded Private SPED Day School (NY Metro)

**Companion to:** `docs/market-research-002-carter-day-school.md`
**Date:** 2026-08-23

---

## The two-customer problem

This product has **one user and two customers**. The student attends. The parent chooses and enrolls. The district pays. A third party — the special-education attorney — controls most of the referral flow and effectively underwrites the parent's willingness to commit.

Every segment below must be evaluated on all three: *will the parent choose us, will the attorney refer to us, will the district pay for us.*

---

## P1 — Represented NYC parent *(primary revenue segment)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Professional or dual-income household. Manhattan Upper West Side, midtown/lower Manhattan, or brownstone Brooklyn — the corridors where Carter density runs **14.8 and 6.7 cases per 1,000 students** against **under 1.0 in Queens**. Child in grades 1–5 with dyslexia, dysgraphia, or a language-based learning disability, often with co-occurring ADHD or executive-function needs |
| **Pain** | The district's IEP offers ICT or a 12:1:1 that has produced flat reading data for two consecutive years. The parent has a private neuropsychological evaluation that says something different from what the school says |
| **Current solution** | Already retained counsel; may already be in a private placement or preparing to file |
| **Trigger event** | A second year of flat progress data; a denied ESY recommendation; a neuropsychologist explicitly recommending a specialized setting; an older sibling's successful case |
| **Willingness to pay** | **$78K–$100K**, anchored to the **$101,757 average settlement**. Price sensitivity is low; *risk* sensitivity is high |
| **What they are actually buying** | Not instruction. **A defensible placement** — a school that will hold up under cross-examination, and a head of school who will testify convincingly |
| **Where they are** | Attorney referral (dominant), neuropsychologist referral, parent Facebook and WhatsApp groups, school-tour circuits |
| **Decision maker** | **Parent decides. Attorney gatekeeps.** Attorneys will not refer to a school that loses prong two |
| **Objection to expect** | *"You have no track record. Why would we risk our case on a first-year school?"* |
| **Answer** | The Dean's district-side CSE tenure as testifying witness; documented methodology; baseline-and-progress data collected from day one; and a first cohort deliberately seeded with pendency continuers who carry no appropriateness risk |

## P2 — Pendency continuer *(the launch segment)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | A family that has **already prevailed** and holds a district-funded placement, now needing to move — their school is closing, the grade band is ending, or the placement has become a poor fit |
| **Pain** | Any transfer risks disrupting pendency and reopening a settled fight |
| **Current solution** | Currently district-funded at another private school |
| **Trigger event** | School closure or restructuring; child ages out of a grade band; documented mismatch |
| **Willingness to pay** | Same as P1, **with zero collection risk** — the funding stream already exists |
| **Strategic value** | **This is how you open the school.** P2 families arrive already funded, already represented, already past the appropriateness question. They de-risk cash flow *and* build the win record that unlocks P1 |
| **Where they are** | Attorney networks, school-transition counselors, parent networks at closing or contracting schools |
| **Decision maker** | Parent + attorney, jointly |
| **Priority** | **Recruit P2 first. Target 50–60% of cohort one.** |

## P3 — Unrepresented Queens / western Nassau parent *(the strategic segment)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Middle-income, frequently first-generation advocate, often Black or Latino — the families who are **~75% of the public-school disability population but only 24% of tuition-payment recipients**. Child has an IEP and no meaningful progress |
| **Pain** | Two layers: the child is not learning, *and* the parent does not know a funded private placement is legally possible |
| **Current solution** | Nothing. Or private tutoring at $100–200/hr they cannot sustain |
| **Trigger event** | Learning the pathway exists — from a workshop, a community organization, or another parent |
| **Willingness to pay** | **$0 up front.** Not a discount question. They cannot write the August check, and Vincent Smith's published position — *"does not offer financial aid or scholarships"* — tells them so directly |
| **What they need** | A direct-payment (*Connors*-type) structure, an attorney referral the school helps arrange, and someone to explain the process in plain language |
| **Where they are** | INCLUDEnyc, Legal Aid, community-based organizations, church and library IEP workshops, **and the existing IEP & Thrive summer-program parent base** |
| **Decision maker** | Parent |
| **Strategic value** | This is the segment the incumbents structurally exclude, the answer to the equity critique that will drive reform, and the reason this school survives a policy shift that targets schools serving only affluent families |
| **Cost** | **Working capital.** Each P3 seat is a ~$76,500 receivable carried through a ~152-day resolution cycle plus settlement-to-payment lag |
| **Priority** | Deliberate, capped, and measured. **Do not open a P3 seat you have not financed.** |

## P4 — Long Island district-funded family *(the founder's warm channel)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Nassau or Suffolk family whose district concedes at a resolution session rather than litigate, or places by agreement |
| **Pain** | District genuinely has no in-district program for a language-based LD profile |
| **Current solution** | Existing LI placements — Vincent Smith, Winston Prep |
| **Trigger event** | CSE concedes; district SPED director seeks a placement |
| **Willingness to pay** | District pays; no family cash risk |
| **Where they are** | **CSE chairs and district SPED directors — Tiffany Lozado's existing professional network** |
| **Decision maker** | **The district, not the parent** |
| **Note** | Volume here is thin. Non-NYC due-process filings across the entire rest of New York State were **358 and declining**. Real, warm, and small — treat as a supplement, not a base. It also builds precisely the district relationships a future Door A regional-need determination would require |

## Anti-personas — who we should not pursue

- **Intensive autism / 1:1 medical-model students.** Average settlements near **$144,000**, but the staffing, clinical, and liability profile is an entirely different school. Do not chase the higher price into a business we are not built to run.
- **Severe emotional and behavioral needs.** Belongs in an 853 residential or District 75 setting.
- **Families seeking a general-education private school with support.** Weak prong-two posture; they will lose, and the loss attaches to our record.
- **Families with no IEP and no evaluation.** No case, no funding pathway, and pursuing them muddies the school's positioning.

## What every segment is actually buying

Not small classes. Not Orton-Gillingham. Those are table stakes across every competitor.

They are buying **the probability that the district pays** — and that probability is a function of the school's evidentiary record and the credibility of the person who testifies. A career Dean who has written IEPs, chaired CSE meetings, and can explain to a hearing officer exactly why the district's program was inadequate for *this* child **is the product**. Everything else is delivery.
